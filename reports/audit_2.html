A constantly running toilet is a common household plumbing issue that wastes water and can lead to higher utility bills. The most frequent culprits include: * **Faulty Flapper** The rubber flapper at the bottom of the tank lifts to allow water into the bowl when flushed and then seals the opening. If it's worn, warped, cracked, or has mineral buildup, it won't seal properly, letting water leak into the bowl. This is the most common cause and often the easiest and cheapest to fix. * **Improper Float Height** The float (either a ball on an arm or a cup around the fill valve) signals the fill valve to stop filling the tank when the water reaches a certain level. If the float is set too high, the water level will rise above the overflow tube, causing water to continuously spill into it and down into the bowl. * **Faulty Fill Valve** Also known as the ballcock, the fill valve controls the water flowing into the tank after a flush. If it's malfunctioning, clogged with sediment, or worn out, it may not shut off properly, leading to a constant flow of water. You might hear a constant hissing sound if the fill valve is the issue. * **Tangled or Misadjusted Chain** The chain connects the flush handle to the flapper. If it's too tight, too loose, or tangled, it can prevent the flapper from sealing correctly. * **Mispositioned Refill Tube** The small refill tube directs water into the overflow tube to refill the bowl after a flush. If it's positioned too low or submerged in the water, it can siphon water out of the tank, causing the fill valve to cycle continuously. **How to Diagnose and Fix a Running Toilet:** Before starting, turn off the water supply to the toilet using the shut-off valve located on the wall behind it. Flush the toilet to empty the tank. 1. **Check the Flapper:** * Inspect the flapper for any visible signs of wear, cracks, warping, or mineral deposits. * Ensure the flapper sits flat and creates a proper seal over the flush valve opening. * **Food Coloring Test:** Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank water. Wait 20-30 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper isn't sealing correctly and needs to be cleaned or replaced. * **Fix:** Clean any grime or mineral buildup from the flapper and its seat. Untangle any knots. 3. **Check and Adjust the Float:** * The water level in the tank should typically be about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. If it's higher, water will continuously spill into the overflow tube. * **Fix:** For ball-style floats, gently bend the float arm downwards.
